## Essential Problem:

The essential problem is that some countries with nuclear power plants are not running them at full capacity, leading to higher emissions from fossil fuel power plants. This raises the question of why this is happening and whether it would be better to increase nuclear power generation to reduce emissions.

    * **Political considerations:** Are there public concerns or policy restrictions limiting nuclear power generation?

## Draft Answer:

While nuclear power offers a low-carbon energy source, several factors can contribute to under-utilisation of nuclear power plants:

* **Technical limitations:** Maintenance needs, safety concerns, or technical issues might restrict plant operation.
* **Economic considerations:** Cheaper energy sources like renewables or fossil fuels with government subsidies could make nuclear power less competitive.
* **Political factors:** Public opposition, policy restrictions, or a shift towards renewable energy targets could limit nuclear power generation.

Furthermore, integrating nuclear power into a grid with high renewable energy penetration can be challenging due to the intermittency of renewables. This might require adjustments to grid infrastructure and energy storage solutions.

The carbon footprint of exported energy needs careful evaluation. While Denmark's exports might have a higher carbon footprint than nuclear power, the overall impact on emissions depends on the specific situation and the emissions avoided by using nuclear power instead of fossil fuels in Sweden.

Finally, increasing nuclear power generation requires addressing concerns about waste management and potential safety risks. These issues need to be transparently communicated and addressed with robust safety measures and responsible waste disposal strategies.
