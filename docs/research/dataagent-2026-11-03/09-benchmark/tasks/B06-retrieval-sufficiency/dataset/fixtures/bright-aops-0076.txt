## Step 1: Identify the essential problem.

The essential problem is to find the sum of two infinite geometric series with the same first term but different common ratios. 

## Step 2: Think step by step to reason and describe what information could be relevant and helpful to address the questions in detail.

1. **Finding the formula for the sum of an infinite geometric series:**

We know that the sum of an infinite geometric series with first term $a$ and common ratio $r$ is given by $\frac{a}{1-r}$ (provided $|r|<1$). 

2. **Applying the formula to the given series:**

We can apply this formula to each of the given series to find their respective sums. For the first series, we have $r_1 = \frac{a}{1-r_1}$. For the second series, we have $r_2 = \frac{a}{1-r_2}$.

3. **Adding the two equations:**

Adding the two equations, we obtain $r_1 + r_2 = \frac{a}{1-r_1} + \frac{a}{1-r_2}$. 

4. **Simplifying the expression:**

To simplify the expression, we can find a common denominator and combine the fractions. 

## Step 3: Draft an answer with as many thoughts as you have.

Combining the fractions, we get:

$$r_1 + r_2 = \frac{a(1-r_2) + a(1-r_1)}{(1-r_1)(1-r_2)}.$$

Expanding the numerator and simplifying, we obtain:

$$r_1 + r_2 = \frac{2a - ar_1 - ar_2}{1 - r_1 - r_2 + r_1r_2}.$$

We know that $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the sums of the two infinite geometric series, which means they are positive numbers. Therefore, $1 - r_1 - r_2 + r_1r_2 > 0$.

Since $r_1$ and $r_2$ are positive, we can divide both sides of the equation by $2a$:

$$\frac{r_1 + r_2}{2a} = \frac{1 - \frac{r_1}{2} - \frac{r_2}{2} + \frac{r_1r_2}{2a}}{1 - r_1 - r_2 + r_1r_2}.$$

Noticing that $\frac{r_1}{2}$ and $\frac{r_2}{2}$ are the means of $r_1$ and $0$, and $r_2$ and $0$ respectively, we can apply the AM-GM inequality:

$$\frac{r_1 + r_2}{2a} \ge \sqrt{\left(\frac{r_1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{r_2}{2}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{r_1r_2}{4a}}.$$

Therefore,

$$r_1 + r_2 \ge 2\sqrt{\frac{ar_1r_2}{4a}} = \sqrt{r_1r_2}.$$

Since $r_1$ and $r_2$ are positive, we can square both sides:

$$(r_1 + r_2)^2 \ge r_1r_2.$$

Therefore, the minimum value of $r_1 + r_2$ is $\sqrt{r_1r_2}$. This minimum value occurs when $r_1 = r_2$. 

However, the problem states that the two series have different common ratios. Therefore, $r_1 + r_2$ must be greater than $\sqrt{r_1r_2}$.

$$r_1 + r_2 = \frac{1 + \sqrt {5}}{2} > \sqrt{r_1r_2}.$$
