## Relevant information and reasoning:

To address your question, we need to consider several factors:

**1. Genetics:**

* Each child inherits a unique set of genes from their parents. These genes influence various aspects of their development, including physical traits, personality, and cognitive abilities.
* While genes play a significant role, they don't solely determine behavior. There's a level of randomness in gene expression, and genes often interact with environmental factors in complex ways.

**2. Environment:**

* The environment encompasses everything that surrounds and influences an individual, including their physical surroundings, social interactions, and cultural experiences.
* From the moment they are born, children are exposed to different environments that shape their development and behavior. Even in the controlled setting of an empty room, they would still have individual experiences (e.g., how they were handled, fed, etc.) that would influence their behavior.

**3. Developmental Stage:**

* The age of the children is crucial. Newborn babies have limited behavioral repertoire and rely heavily on innate reflexes and responses. As they grow older, their behavior becomes more complex and influenced by learning and experience.

**4. Individual Differences:**

* Even children with similar genetic backgrounds and environmental exposures may exhibit different behaviors due to individual variations in temperament, personality, and cognitive abilities.
