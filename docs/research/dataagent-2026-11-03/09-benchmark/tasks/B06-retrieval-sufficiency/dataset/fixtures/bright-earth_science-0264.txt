## The Prime Meridian: Shifting Ground, but Steady Position

## 1. The Essential Problem:

The Earth's tectonic plates are constantly moving, which means the physical location of the Prime Meridian marked at the Greenwich Observatory is also shifting. This raises questions about the meridian's precise location and its implications for navigation and global positioning systems.

## 2. Relevant Information:

* **Tectonic Plate Movement:** The Eurasian Plate, on which the Greenwich Observatory stands, moves about 2.5 cm eastwards annually. 
* **Prime Meridian Definition:** It's not defined by a physical marker but by the Earth's rotation and celestial observations.
* **International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS):** This organization monitors Earth's rotation and updates the coordinates of the Prime Meridian.
* **Global Positioning System (GPS):** Relies on a network of satellites and ground stations to determine location, referencing the IERS-maintained coordinates of the Prime Meridian.

## 3. Draft Answer:

While the physical marker at the Greenwich Observatory is shifting due to plate movement, **the Prime Meridian itself remains fixed at 0° 0' 0"**. This is because the meridian is defined by the Earth's rotation and not by any physical point on the surface. 

The IERS constantly monitors the Earth's rotation and adjusts the coordinates of the Prime Meridian accordingly. This ensures that GPS and other navigation systems maintain accuracy despite the movement of the Eurasian Plate.

Therefore, if your GPS says you are 100 km west of Greenwich Observatory, you are indeed 100 km west of the **true** Prime Meridian, not the physical marker on the ground. This distinction is crucial for accurate positioning and navigation.

**Implications:**

* The movement of the Greenwich Observatory marker has minimal impact on daily life as the true Prime Meridian remains fixed.
* The IERS plays a vital role in maintaining the accuracy of global positioning systems by updating the coordinates of the Prime Meridian.
* Understanding the difference between the physical marker and the true Prime Meridian is essential for accurate interpretation of GPS data.

**Who Decides?:**

The International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS), a global collaboration of scientists and organizations, is responsible for defining and maintaining the coordinates of the Prime Meridian.

**Additional Notes:**

* The movement of the Eurasian Plate is minuscule compared to the Earth's circumference, making the impact on the Prime Meridian's location negligible.
* GPS technology considers the Earth's rotation and adjusts its calculations accordingly, ensuring that the reported positions are accurate despite the movement of the Greenwich Observatory marker.
