## Identifying the essential problem:

The essential problem is to find the minimum number of questions needed to identify a specific card out of 256 in a deck, where the only information available is the answer to the question "Is the card in the first half of the remaining deck?".

## Reasoning step by step:

1. **Initial division:** We start by dividing the deck of 256 cards into two equal halves of 128 cards each. We ask if the card is in the first half.
2. **Halving the options:** Based on the answer, we know the card is either in the first 128 cards or the second 128 cards. This eliminates half of the possibilities.
3. **Repeating the process:** We repeat the process of dividing the remaining cards in half and asking if the card is in the first half. With each question, we eliminate half of the remaining possibilities.
4. **Reaching the final card:** We continue this process until we are left with only one card remaining. This card must be the chosen card.

## Information relevant to the questions:

1. **Number of cards in the deck:** Knowing the total number of cards (256) is crucial for understanding the scope of the problem.
2. **Dividing the deck in half:** Dividing the deck in half ensures that we consistently eliminate half of the remaining possibilities with each question.
3. **Answer to the question:** The answer to the question "Is the card in the first half?" provides information about the location of the card within the remaining deck.

## Drafting an answer:

To identify a specific card out of 256, we need to ask **8 questions**. This is because each question eliminates half of the remaining possibilities, and we need to narrow down the options from 256 to 1.
