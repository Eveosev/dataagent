## Relevant Information

To address your questions effectively, let's gather relevant information:

**1. Existing Resources:**

* **Community Solar:** This model allows individuals to subscribe to a shared solar farm instead of installing panels on their own roof. Several companies offer community solar programs in various states, including Pennsylvania.
* **Solar Leases:** Some companies offer solar leases where you pay a monthly fee for the panels installed on your roof. While not technically "renting" panels in another state, it provides access to solar without upfront costs.
* **Renewable Energy Investment Platforms:** Platforms like Mosaic and Sunfunder allow individuals to invest directly in solar projects across the country, including those in California and Arizona.

**2. Economic Feasibility:**

* **Community Solar:** The economic feasibility depends on the specific program, electricity rates, and your energy usage. It can be cheaper than traditional electricity in some cases.
* **Solar Leases:**  Leases typically involve long-term contracts with fixed monthly payments, offering predictable costs but potentially limiting long-term savings compared to ownership.
* **Renewable Energy Investment Platforms:** Returns on these platforms vary depending on the project and market conditions. Researching individual projects and understanding the risks involved is crucial.
