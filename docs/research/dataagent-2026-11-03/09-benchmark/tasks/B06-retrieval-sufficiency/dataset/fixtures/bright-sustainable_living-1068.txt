## The True Cost of Raising Chickens for Meat

### 1. Identifying the Essential Problem:

The essential problem is determining the average cost of raising a chicken for meat, considering feed, chicks, and the butchering process.

### 2. Relevant Information and Considerations:

**A. Initial Investment:** While the prompt excludes the initial housing investment, it's worth noting that this can vary significantly depending on the type of coop or setup you choose. A simple DIY coop might cost a few hundred dollars, while a more elaborate pre-built coop could be several thousand.

**B. Chicks:**

* **Cost:** The cost of chicks varies depending on the breed, age, and source. You can expect to pay anywhere from $2-$10 per chick. 
* **Mortality rate:**  It's crucial to factor in a potential mortality rate, which can range from 5% to 10%. This means you might need to purchase a few extra chicks to account for potential losses.

**C. Feed:**

* **Type of feed:** The type of feed you choose will significantly impact the cost. Starter feed for young chicks is more expensive than grower feed for older birds. You may also want to supplement their diet with fresh greens or kitchen scraps to reduce feed costs.
* **Consumption:**  The amount of feed a chicken consumes depends on its age and breed. On average, a chicken will consume around 5 pounds of feed per month.

**D. Butchering:**

* **DIY vs. Professional:** Butchering your own chickens can save on costs but requires specific equipment and knowledge. Professional butchering services will add to the overall cost.
* **Processing costs:** Additional processing costs like packaging and freezing may also apply.

**E. Other Considerations:**

* **Time commitment:** Raising chickens requires daily care, including feeding, watering, cleaning the coop, and monitoring their health. 
* **Veterinary care:**  While chickens are generally hardy animals, unexpected illnesses or injuries could necessitate veterinary care, adding to the cost.
* **Opportunity cost:** Consider the time and effort invested in raising chickens. This could represent an opportunity cost compared to other activities.


### 3. Draft Answer:

Based on the information above, let's break down the approximate cost per pound of raising a chicken for meat:

**A. Chicks:** Assume $3 per chick and a 10% mortality rate. 
* Cost per surviving chick = $3 / (1-0.10) = $3.33

**B. Feed:** Assuming 5 pounds of feed per month at $0.50 per pound for 5 months (typical grow-out period). 
* Feed cost per chicken = 5 months * 5 pounds/month * $0.50/pound = $12.50

**C. Butchering:** Assuming a DIY approach to keep costs low.

**D. Other considerations:** 

* Time commitment: This is difficult to quantify but should be factored into the overall cost.
* Veterinary care: Budget a small amount for potential veterinary needs.

**Total cost per chicken:** $3.33 (chicks) + $12.50 (feed) + $0 (butchering) + $1 (veterinary) = $16.83

**Average weight of a broiler chicken:** 5 pounds

**Cost per pound of chicken:** $16.83 / 5 pounds = $3.37 per pound 

**Therefore, the average cost of raising a chicken for meat, excluding the initial housing investment, is approximately $3.37 per pound.**

**Important notes:**

* This is an approximate estimate, and actual costs can vary depending on several factors, including breed, feed costs, and butchering methods. 
* The cost per pound can decrease if you raise a larger number of chickens, as fixed costs like housing are spread across more birds.
* Raising chickens for meat can be a rewarding experience, providing you with fresh, high-quality meat and potentially reducing your reliance on commercially produced poultry. However, it's crucial to consider the costs and commitment involved before starting your flock.
