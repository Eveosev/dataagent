## Essential Problem

The essential problem is to determine whether assigning a unique number to each intersection in a city's road map, where each street is one-way and leads from a lower-numbered intersection to a higher-numbered one, guarantees that there are no routes allowing you to start from one intersection and return to it without retracing your steps.

## Relevant Information

* **One-way streets:** This implies that you can only travel in one direction on each street.
* **Unique intersection numbers:** Each intersection has a distinct number, and these numbers increase as you move along a street.
* **No retracing steps:** The question asks if it's possible to return to your starting intersection without traveling on the same street twice.

## Step-by-Step Reasoning

1. **Consider a single loop:** If there's a loop of streets that starts and ends at the same intersection, you can travel around the loop without violating the one-way rule. This would allow you to return to your starting point without retracing your steps.

2. **Break down the map into components:** Imagine dividing the map into smaller sections based on intersections with multiple outgoing streets. Analyze each section individually to see if it contains any loops.

3. **Identify dead ends:** If there are any dead ends (intersections with only one incoming street), then there's no way to return to the starting point from those intersections without retracing your steps.

4. **Analyze intersections with multiple exits:** For intersections with multiple outgoing streets, consider the direction of the streets and the numbering system. If all outgoing streets lead to higher-numbered intersections, then there's no way to return to the starting point without retracing your steps.

5. **Overall connectivity:** Consider the overall connectivity of the map. If all intersections are connected in a single network, then the presence of loops becomes more likely.

## Draft Answer

Based on the analysis above, the statement is **False**. While assigning unique numbers and ensuring one-way streets with increasing numbers helps prevent retracing steps on individual streets, it does not guarantee the absence of loops in the overall map. The presence of loops allows for routes that start and end at the same intersection without retracing steps.

Therefore, it is possible for a city's road map with the given conditions to have routes that allow you to return to your starting intersection without retracing your steps. However, this depends on the specific layout of the map and the presence of loops.
