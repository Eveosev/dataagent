## Essential Problem:

The size of Zealandia compared to other continents is raising questions about whether it actually qualifies as a continent or if it's simply a continental fragment.

## Relevant Information:

* **Zealandia's size:** Its landmass above sea level is about 268,000 square kilometers, which is significantly smaller than the smallest continent, Australia, at 7.69 million square kilometers.
* **Zealandia's geology:** It shares the same continental crust composition as Australia and Antarctica, indicating a shared origin. 
* **Zealandia's elevation and definition:** Most of its landmass is submerged, with only 5% above sea level. However, it meets the other criteria for a continent, having a distinctive geology, a well-defined area, and a crust thicker than the ocean floor.

## Addressing the Questions:

**1. Criteria for a continent:** 

While there's no universally agreed-upon definition, the basic criteria for a continent typically include:

* **Landmass size:** Continents are generally large landmasses compared to islands or smaller landforms. However, there's no specific minimum size requirement.
* **Continental crust:** Continents have a crust composed of thick, low-density rocks like granite, unlike the thinner, denser oceanic crust.
* **Elevation:** Although not always the case, continents usually have a significant portion of their landmass above sea level.
* **Geological distinctiveness:** Continents have a unique geological history and structure, different from surrounding landforms.

**2. Does Zealandia meet the criteria?**

- **Landmass size:** Zealandia falls short compared to other continents in terms of size.
- **Continental crust:** It fulfills this criteria, possessing the same continental crust type as its neighboring continents.
- **Elevation:** Though largely submerged, the portion above sea level is well-defined and geologically distinct.
- **Geological distinctiveness:** Zealandia's shared geological history and unique features solidify its distinctiveness.

**3. Continental fragment vs. continent:**

Given the existing criteria, Zealandia's classification becomes debatable. 

* **Arguments for continent:** Despite its size, it exhibits all other characteristics: continental crust, a well-defined area, high elevation in some parts, and geological distinctiveness. 
* **Arguments for continental fragment:** Its submerged state and smaller size raise questions about whether it fully qualifies as a separate continent or a broken fragment of a larger one.

**Conclusion:**

Zealandia presents a unique case. While its smaller size compared to other continents raises questions, it fulfills most other criteria for continental classification. Ultimately, whether it's considered a full continent or a continental fragment remains a subject of ongoing scientific debate and discussion.
