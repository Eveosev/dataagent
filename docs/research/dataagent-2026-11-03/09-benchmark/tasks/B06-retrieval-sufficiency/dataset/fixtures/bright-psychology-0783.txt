## Essential Problem:

The essential problem is to understand the social and health outcomes of individuals who climb the social ladder, specifically in terms of life expectancy, divorce rates, and happiness. 

## Relevant Information:

To address this question, we need to consider the following:

* **Definition of "climbing the social ladder":** How do we define upward mobility? Is it purely based on income, or do we consider other factors like education, occupation, and social status?
* **Timeframe:** When did these individuals climb the social ladder? Did they experience upward mobility in childhood, adulthood, or later in life? 
* **Original social position:** What was their starting point on the social ladder?  
* **Specific health and social outcomes:** We need to consider various factors like life expectancy, divorce rates, mental and physical health, and overall well-being.
* **Longitudinal studies:**  Longitudinal studies that track individuals over time are crucial to understand the impact of social mobility on their lives. 

## Research and Available Data:

Several studies have investigated the link between social mobility and health and social outcomes. Here are some key findings:

* **Life Expectancy:** A study published in the Journal of Gerontology found that individuals who experienced upward mobility had a higher life expectancy than those who remained at the bottom of the social ladder. 
* **Divorce Rates:** Research suggests that individuals who climb the social ladder may have lower divorce rates compared to those who stay at the bottom. However, this might be confounded by other factors like education and income. 
* **Happiness:** Studies have shown that upward mobility can lead to increased happiness and well-being. However, the long-term effects on happiness are less clear.

## Addressing the Specific Questions:

Based on the available research, it seems that individuals who climb the social ladder experience **health and social outcomes more similar to those at the top of the social hierarchy than those at the bottom.** However, it's important to remember that these are general trends, and individual experiences can vary depending on various factors.

## Further Research:

More research is needed to understand the nuances of social mobility and its impact on individuals' lives. This includes:

* **Longitudinal studies:**  Investigating the long-term effects of upward mobility on health and social outcomes.
* **Qualitative studies:**  Exploring the lived experiences of individuals who have climbed the social ladder.
* **Comparative studies:**  Comparing the experiences of individuals who achieved upward mobility through different pathways (e.g., education, career advancement).

By understanding the complex relationship between social mobility and health and social outcomes, we can develop policies and interventions to support individuals who are moving up the social ladder and ensure they have the opportunity to live healthy and fulfilling lives.
