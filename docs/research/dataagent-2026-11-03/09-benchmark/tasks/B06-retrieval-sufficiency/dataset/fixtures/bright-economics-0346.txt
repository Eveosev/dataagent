## The Drop in CEO Pay Around 2000: A Multifaceted Issue

The observed decrease in CEO pay around the year 2000, despite the general upward trend, presents an interesting anomaly. While the book you're reading attributes rising CEO pay to the separation of ownership and control, the specific dip in the early 2000s likely resulted from a confluence of factors. 

### Potential Contributing Factors:

1. **Economic Downturn:** The dot-com bubble burst and the subsequent recession of 2001 likely played a significant role. Shareholder value plummeted, impacting companies' ability and willingness to maintain high CEO compensation packages. 
2. **Increased Scrutiny and Regulation:** The late 1990s and early 2000s saw increased public and regulatory scrutiny of executive compensation, fueled by concerns over excessive pay packages. This scrutiny could have led to a temporary correction in CEO pay levels.
3. **Shifting Corporate Governance Practices:** The adoption of stricter corporate governance practices, including stronger board oversight and greater shareholder activism, might have contributed to a reining in of CEO pay.
4. **Changes in Tax Policy:** Modifications in tax policies related to executive compensation could have disincentivized overly generous pay packages.
5. **Focus on Long-Term Performance:** A growing emphasis on long-term performance metrics rather than short-term gains might have led to a shift in compensation structures, potentially resulting in a temporary dip in immediate CEO pay.

It's important to note that these factors likely interacted and influenced each other, making it difficult to pinpoint a single definitive cause for the observed decrease.

By delving deeper into these areas, we can gain a more comprehensive understanding of the complex dynamics behind the temporary decline in CEO pay around the year 2000.
