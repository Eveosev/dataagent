## Essential Problem:

The essential problem is the environmental impact of palm oil production, specifically deforestation, forest fires, and biodiversity loss. The user wants to understand the environmental friendliness of certified organic palm oil compared to RSPO-certified and non-certified palm oil. 

## Relevant Information:

* **Palm oil production and its environmental impact:** Palm oil is a versatile and widely used vegetable oil, but its production has been linked to deforestation, habitat loss, and greenhouse gas emissions. 
* **RSPO certification:** The Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil (RSPO) is a set of environmental and social standards for the sustainable production of palm oil. While not perfect, RSPO certification is generally considered better than non-certified palm oil.
* **Organic certification:** Organic certification in the EU ensures that the palm oil is produced without synthetic pesticides, fertilizers, or genetically modified organisms (GMOs). However, organic certification does not guarantee sustainability in terms of deforestation or biodiversity loss.

## Comparing Different Types of Palm Oil:

**1. Non-certified palm oil:** This is the least environmentally friendly option, as it has no guarantee of sustainable production practices. 

**2. RSPO-certified palm oil:** This is a step up from non-certified palm oil, as it adheres to some environmental and social standards. However, the RSPO has been criticized for its lack of effectiveness in preventing deforestation and human rights abuses.

**3. Certified organic palm oil:** This type of palm oil meets the EU's organic standards, which means it is produced without harmful chemicals. However, organic certification does not guarantee that the palm oil is deforestation-free or produced in a way that protects biodiversity. 

**Therefore, while certified organic palm oil might be better than non-certified palm oil in terms of chemical use, it is not necessarily a more sustainable option when it comes to deforestation and biodiversity loss.**

## Additional Considerations:

* **Traceability:** When choosing palm oil products, it is important to look for products with traceable supply chains. This allows consumers to know where the palm oil comes from and how it was produced.
* **Alternatives:** Whenever possible, it is best to avoid palm oil altogether and opt for alternative vegetable oils with lower environmental impacts, such as sunflower oil or rapeseed oil.

## Conclusion:

While certified organic palm oil might be a better choice than non-certified palm oil in some aspects, it is important to remember that it is not a guarantee of sustainability. Consumers should look for products with traceable supply chains and consider alternative oils whenever possible to minimize their environmental impact.
