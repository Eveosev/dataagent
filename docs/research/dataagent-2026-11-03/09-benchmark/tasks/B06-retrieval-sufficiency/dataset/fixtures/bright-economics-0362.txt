## Essential Problem:

The essential problem is that the standard DID estimator is biased when the parallel trends assumption is violated, even if the violation is small. In the specific case presented, wages in OH are higher than wages in IN for a short period before the treatment. This pre-existing difference can lead to an overestimation of the treatment effect if not accounted for.

## Relevant Information:

* **Nature of the Pre-existing Difference:** We need to understand the nature of the pre-existing difference and its potential causes. Is it a one-time event or a persistent trend? Is it related to the treatment in any way?
* **Magnitude of the Pre-existing Difference:** The magnitude of the pre-existing difference relative to the expected treatment effect is crucial. A small difference might not significantly impact the results, while a large difference could render the DID estimates unreliable.
* **Data Availability:** The availability of data for periods before the treatment is essential. Longer pre-treatment periods provide more information about the trends and allow for a more robust analysis.

## Potential Solutions:

* **Controlling for Pre-existing Differences:** We can include additional control variables in the DID model to account for the pre-existing difference. These variables could be time-varying factors that explain the difference in wages between OH and IN before the treatment.
* **Using Alternative Estimators:** Several alternative estimators have been proposed to address violations of the parallel trends assumption. These include the synthetic control method, the event study approach, and the difference-in-differences-in-differences (DDD) estimator.
* **Sensitivity Analysis:** Conducting a sensitivity analysis can help assess the impact of the pre-existing difference on the DID estimates. This involves varying the assumptions about the pre-existing difference and observing how the results change.
