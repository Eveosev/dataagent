## Identifying the essential problem

The essential problem is to determine the minimum wavelength of light in nanometers needed to trigger a color change in the germanium glass at room temperature (300 K).

## Relevant information

To solve this problem, we need to consider the following information:

* **Material:** Germanium
* **Property:** Color change upon exposure to light of specific wavelengths
* **Room temperature:** 300 K
* **Concept:** Energy of a photon is inversely proportional to its wavelength
* **Formula:** E = hc/λ, where:
    * E is the energy of a photon
    * h is Planck's constant (6.63 × 10^-34 J s)
    * c is the speed of light (3 × 10^8 m/s)
    * λ is the wavelength of light

## Step-by-step solution

1. **Determine the minimum energy required to trigger the color change in germanium.** This information should be available from the material properties of germanium or from the specific experiment you are conducting. Let's call this minimum energy E_min.
2. **Use the formula E = hc/λ to find the minimum wavelength λ_min.** Rearrange the formula to solve for λ_min: λ_min = hc/E_min.
3. **Plug in the values for h, c, and E_min.** Make sure to convert E_min to Joules if it is given in another unit.
4. **Calculate λ_min and convert the result to nanometers.**
