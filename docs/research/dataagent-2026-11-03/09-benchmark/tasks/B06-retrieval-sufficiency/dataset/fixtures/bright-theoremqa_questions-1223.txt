## Analyzing Musical Tones:

### 1. Identifying the Problem:

The problem is to identify the mathematical representation of two distinct tones in terms of their base frequency. We know that the first tone oscillates at three times the base frequency, while the second oscillates at five times the base frequency and starts with a delay. We need to find the multipliers of the base frequency for each tone.

### 2. Step-by-Step Reasoning:

* **Base Frequency:** Let's denote the base frequency as "f".
* **First Tone:** This tone oscillates at three times the base frequency. Therefore, its frequency is 3f.
* **Second Tone:** This tone oscillates at five times the base frequency but starts later. The "starting later" part implies a phase difference compared to the first tone.
    * Frequency: The frequency of the second tone is 5f.
    * Phase Difference:  We need additional information about the delay to quantify the phase difference.  

### 3. Drafting an Answer:

Based on the information provided, the multipliers for each tone are:

* **First Tone:** 3
* **Second Tone:** 5

However, to fully characterize the second tone, we need additional information about the delay. This information would be crucial in determining the phase difference between the two tones, allowing for a more complete mathematical representation. 

**Further Considerations:**

* The specific mathematical representation of the tones would depend on the chosen model. For example, a simple sinusoidal model could be used, where the amplitude, frequency, and phase are the relevant parameters.
* Additional information about the musical instrument and the context of the tones might be helpful in refining the analysis.
