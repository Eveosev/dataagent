## The Problem:

We need to form a team of 2 marketing specialists and 3 product designers from a pool of 6 marketing specialists and 8 product designers. The question is: how many different teams can we form?

##  Step-by-Step Reasoning:

Here's how we can approach this problem step-by-step:

1. **Combinations, not permutations:** Since the order in which we choose the team members doesn't matter (a team with Sarah and John is the same as a team with John and Sarah), we're dealing with combinations, not permutations.
2. **Choosing the marketing specialists:** We need to choose 2 marketing specialists out of 6. This can be calculated using the combination formula:

nCr = n! / (r! * (n-r)!)

where n is the total number of marketing specialists (6), and r is the number we need to choose (2).

6C2 = 6! / (2! * (6-2)!) = 6! / (2! * 4!) = (6*5) / (2*1) = 15

Therefore, there are 15 ways to choose 2 marketing specialists.
3. **Choosing the product designers:** Similarly, we need to choose 3 product designers out of 8. Using the same formula:

8C3 = 8! / (3! * (8-3)!) = 8! / (3! * 5!) = (8*7*6) / (3*2*1) = 56

There are 56 ways to choose 3 product designers.
4. **Total number of teams:** To find the total number of possible teams, we simply multiply the number of ways to choose marketing specialists by the number of ways to choose product designers:

Total teams = 15 * 56 = 840

Therefore, there are 840 different teams that can be formed.

## Additional thoughts:

* This solution assumes that all team members are distinct and interchangeable within their respective roles.
* If there were specific skills or experience required for each team member, the problem would become more complex and might require additional information.
* Alternative approaches, such as using factorials or recursion, could also be used to solve this problem.
