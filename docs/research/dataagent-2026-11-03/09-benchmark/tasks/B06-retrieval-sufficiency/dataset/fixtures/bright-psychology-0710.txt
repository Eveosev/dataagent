## Essential Problem:

The essential problem is to understand the relative contributions of implicit gender bias and priming effects in influencing responses to the "surgeon operating on my son" riddle. This involves isolating the priming effect from the societal expectations effect.

## Relevant Information:

* **Priming effect:** Exposure to certain words or concepts can subconsciously activate related concepts and influence subsequent thoughts and actions.
* **Implicit gender bias:** Unconscious beliefs and attitudes about gender roles that can influence our perceptions and judgments.
* **Riddle variations:** 
    * **Original:** Father and son in a car crash, surgeon says "I can't operate, this is my son."
    * **Mother and daughter:** Mother and daughter in a car crash, surgeon says "I can't operate, this is my daughter."
    * **Father and daughter:** Father and daughter in a car crash, surgeon says "I can't operate, this is my daughter."
    * **Mother and daughter, father is nurse:** Mother and daughter in a car crash, nurse says "I can't help, this is my daughter."

    * Compare response times to the original riddle and the mother-daughter variation.

    * Compare response times to the father-daughter and mother-daughter variations.
    * If societal expectations are the primary driver, response times should be slower for the father-daughter variation where the surgeon's gender contradicts the expected "father" role.
3. **Quantifying the effects:**
    * Calculate the difference in response times between the original riddle and the variations.
    * This will provide a measure of the relative strength of the priming and societal expectations effects.
