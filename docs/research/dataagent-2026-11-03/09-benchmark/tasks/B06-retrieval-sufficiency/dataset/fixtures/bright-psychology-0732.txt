## Identifying the Bias: 

The bias you describe aligns closely with several cognitive biases, each contributing to the overall phenomenon:

* **Dunning-Kruger effect:** This bias describes how individuals with limited knowledge in a specific domain tend to overestimate their competence and understanding. Conversely, experts in a field might underestimate their competence relative to others due to their heightened awareness of the complexities and nuances within their field.
* **Curse of Knowledge:** Experts, due to their deep understanding, often struggle to explain complex concepts in a way that novices can easily grasp. This leads them to underestimate the public's understanding of their field and potentially distrust the public's ability to make informed decisions within that domain.
* **In-group bias:** Individuals tend to favor and trust information and perspectives originating from their own social groups or areas of expertise. This can lead to a heightened skepticism towards information coming from outside their field, even if the source is reputable.
* **Confirmation bias:** People tend to seek and interpret information in a way that confirms their existing beliefs. Experts, aware of the shortcomings within their field, might be more susceptible to confirmation bias when encountering information that reinforces their distrust.
