##  Identifying the Essential Problem:

The main problem is to prove the existence and uniqueness of a pure strategy Nash Equilibrium (NE) in a two-person continuous game with diagonally strictly concave payoff functions. The difficulty lies in understanding the concept of diagonal strict concavity and its application in this context.

## Step-by-Step Reasoning:

1. **Understanding Diagonal Strict Concavity:** 
    - This property essentially means that the payoff function of each player is strictly concave along any line segment connecting two points in the joint strategy space where the other player's strategy is fixed. 
    - In simpler terms, if you fix the strategy of one player and look at how the payoff of the other player changes as their strategy varies, the payoff function should be strictly concave. This should hold true for both players.
2. **Relevance of Diagonal Strict Concavity:**
    - This property is crucial because it guarantees that each player has a unique best response to any strategy chosen by the other player. 
    - Intuitively, if a player's payoff function is strictly concave, then they will always prefer to move towards the strategy that maximizes their payoff, and there will be a unique strategy that achieves this.
3. **Proving Existence and Uniqueness of NE:**
    - Given diagonal strict concavity, the existence of a pure strategy NE can be established using Kakutani's Fixed Point Theorem. This theorem guarantees the existence of a fixed point in a continuous function that maps a compact, convex set to itself. 
    - In the context of this game, the best response function of each player can be viewed as a continuous function that maps the strategy space of the other player to their own best response. 
    - The intersection of these best response functions represents the set of Nash Equilibria.
    - Uniqueness of the NE follows from the strictness of the concavity condition. 
    - If the concavity were not strict, there could be multiple points where the best response functions intersect, leading to multiple Nash Equilibria.
