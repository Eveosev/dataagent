## Identifying the Essential Problem:

The essential problem is to determine which of the three gray paint shades is closest to a "true" gray, meaning it lacks any noticeable bias towards a specific color hue like red, green, or blue.

## Gathering Relevant Information:

**Understanding Gray:**
- A true gray is created by mixing red, green, and blue in equal proportions.
- Any deviation from this balance will introduce a slight hue of the dominant color.

**Analyzing the Shades:**
- Shade 1: Equal parts of red, green, and blue - This suggests it might be closest to a true gray.
- Shade 2: More green than red and blue, with red being the least - This indicates a slight green hue.
- Shade 3: Mostly green, with even less red and the same amount of blue as Shade 2 - This suggests an even stronger green hue than Shade 2.

## Reasoning and Drafting an Answer:

Based on the above analysis, we can conclude:

- **Shade 1 is most likely to be the truest gray** because it uses equal parts of all three primary colors.
- **Shade 2 will have a slight green hue** due to the increased amount of green pigment.
- **Shade 3 will have an even more noticeable green hue** than Shade 2 because it contains the most green and the least red.

Therefore, the shade closest to a true gray, lacking any distinct hue, is **Shade 1**.
