## Identifying the Problem:

The essential problem is to determine the initial value of a financial product whose payout depends on the price of a specific stock one year from now. We need to consider the stock's price, its growth rate, market volatility, and the relationship between the payout and the stock price (a negative power).

## Relevant Information:

* **Stock Price:** $0.50
* **Growth Rate:** Fixed, but not specified
* **Market Volatility:** Contributes to price fluctuations
* **Payout:** $1.4 based on a negative power of the stock price
* **Interest Rate:** 3%

## Reasoning and Steps:

1. **Model the Stock Price:** We need to model the stock price one year from now, considering the growth rate and market volatility. This could involve using a geometric Brownian motion model or a similar approach.
2. **Relate Payout to Stock Price:** We need to understand the exact relationship between the payout and the stock price. This involves determining the specific negative power applied to the stock price in the payout formula.
3. **Discount Future Payout:** We need to discount the expected payout of $1.4 back to the present using the given 3% interest rate. This will give us the present value of the future payout.
4. **Equate Present Value to Initial Value:** The initial value of the financial product should be equal to the present value of its expected future payout. This allows us to solve for the initial value.

$S_t = S_0 * e^{(r - σ^2/2)t + σ * Z_t}$

* $r$ is the growth rate
* $σ$ is the market volatility
* $Z_t$ is a standard normal random variable

$P = a * S_t^{-b}$

where:

* $P$ is the payout

$1.4 = a * S_t^{-b}$

$PV = P * e^{-rt}$

where:

* $PV$ is the present value
* $P$ is the future payout ($1.4)
* $r$ is the interest rate (0.03)
* $t$ is the time period (1 year)

4. **Equate Present Value to Initial Value:** The initial value of the financial product is equal to the present value of the expected future payout:

$Initial Value = PV = a * S_t^{-b} * e^{-rt}$
